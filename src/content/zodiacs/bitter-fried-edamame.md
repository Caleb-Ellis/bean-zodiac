Scorched Edamame fry hot and serve fast. The pod blisters, the dashi hits, the daikon goes down sharp at the edge of the bowl.

Edamame is picked young, cooked young, eaten young — there is no waiting stage. Bitter does its discernment up front, in the kombu and the bonito, before the bean ever sees the oil. Fried commits: once the pod is in the pan it cannot be uncommitted.

The risk is a bowl that arrives before the broth has anything to say. A cook who never lets the dashi steep long serves a clean dish and a thin one.
