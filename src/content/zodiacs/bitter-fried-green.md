Tempura green beans dressed in matcha salt belong to the excitable end of the zodiac — quick to the oil, quick off it, and bitter enough at the finish to keep the leap honest.

Green bean moves before it deliberates. Fried collapses the gap between heat and ingredient to nothing. Bitter, in the matcha salt, supplies an edge that tells the cook when to stop.

The danger is the batch fried for its own sake. Bitterness is the corrective: a green bean lifted from oil and left unsalted is only half the dish, and the cook learns to wait for the salt before reaching for the next handful.
