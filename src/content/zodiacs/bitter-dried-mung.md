The desiccated mung bean works by subtraction. What the pod gave up in drying, the cook gives back in measured spoonfuls — never the full handful, never the rough pour.

Mung tends what is depleted; bitter requires the care be earned; dried strips the warmth out of the gesture, leaving the accuracy intact. The methi is counted. The turmeric struck level. The broth rises without surplus, and nothing in the pot is there by accident.

The risk is a kitchen run by the gram. A dal correct to the recipe can still leave the eater hungry for the hand that made it; the desiccated mung bean does well to let a little slack into the measure, and to feed the cook as well as the patient.
