Akara wants its batter beaten until it lifts a spoon — peeled kidney beans pounded with onion and salt, no flour to soften the impact, dropped into oil hot enough to seal the surface before the centre can think. The fritter that goes in tentative comes out heavy.

The pepper sauce is its companion and its argument: scotch bonnet, raw onion, a little oil. It is not poured over; it sits on the side, taken or refused with each bite. The dish offers the heat and lets the eater decide.

Cooked timid, akara turns oily and dull. Cooked direct, the crust shatters and the inside steams open, ready for whatever the sauce brings.
