Black beans dropped into hot oil throw off their skins and crisp at the edge before the centre has caught up. Tomatillos go on the dry pan beside them; bitter greens wilt in the rendered fat. Everything is assembled at the last moment, on a tostada that will not hold for long.

Eat standing, over the pan. The shell goes soft within minutes of the beans landing, and the char on the tomatillo dulls as it cools. What is sharp here is sharp briefly.

Salt at the end, not the beginning. The bitterness of the greens needs somewhere to land, and the bean's crust will draw salt straight through to the soft inside if given the chance too early.
