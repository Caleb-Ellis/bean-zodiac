# Zodiac body-copy style

This governs the **markdown body** of each file in `zodiacs/` — the prose _below_ the
frontmatter. Frontmatter fields (facets, fortunes, dish, quote, `seasonalFortune`) have
their own voice and are out of scope here.

A body is four sentences in one paragraph, and those four sentences are the entry's
`trait` / `excess` / `inverse` in miniature. The form is fixed. What varies is entirely
inside it — the scene, the object, the turn.

> Measured across the 79 approved bodies: **79 of 79 are a single paragraph**, **78 of 79
> are exactly four sentences**, and **none** continues its opening sentence past the trait.
> An earlier version of this document told you to vary length and paragraph count. The
> corpus settled somewhere else, and the corpus is the spec.

---

## The four sentences

**1 — The opener.** `[Preparation] [Bean] are the Bean Zodiac's most [trait].` A full stop
ends it. Don't continue past the trait word with a dash or a clause; no approved body does.
Where "most" reads badly against the trait, the superlative is allowed instead — _"the Bean
Zodiac's grittiest"_, _"the Bean Zodiac's toughest"_ — but that is three entries in 79, so
reach for it only when "most X" is genuinely awkward. Mind the bean's plural: it is
_Hickory Chickpeas_ and _Infused Edamame_, not "Chickpea Beans".

**2 — The trait, as one scene.** What the virtue looks like in a room, doing well. This is
the sentence that earns the entry, and it is where the concrete detail goes: a number, an
object, a moment with an edge on it. _"Three sips and they can tell you which one is worth
the money and why, and they are right."_ Not a summary of the disposition — an instance
of it.

**3 — The excess.** The same quality, one turn past the point where it serves. One sentence,
never two. Usually opens `Sometimes` (46 of 79) or `Occasionally` (8), and the rest lead
with the thing itself — _"The labelling can become the whole evening."_ Vary the opener:
`Sometimes` is the house default, not the house requirement.

**4 — The inverse, as an impossibility.** Front the opposite behaviour as a gerund or a
participial phrase, then say they cannot do it:

> _Sleeping on it_ **is advice they have never once taken.**
> _Not wanting to know_ **is foreign.**
> _Being ordinary company for an evening_ **is beyond them.**
> _Smoothing something over with a kind vagueness_ **they cannot do.**
> _Stating a thing outright_ **would feel like turning the lights on.**

The tail is where the wit lives, so it carries the widest variation in the corpus: _is a
struggle for them_, _is not a risk they run_, _costs them real effort_, _isn't an option
they will take_, _is a pleasure they've mostly given up_, _is not a holiday they take_.
Reach for a fresh one where you can — though a tail recurring a few files apart is normal
and not worth an edit; the approved set does it around a dozen times.

**The one permitted departure:** sentences 3 and 4 may fuse into a single sentence carrying
both poles, giving a three-sentence body. Exactly one approved entry does this
(`bitter-boiled-butter`) and it works because the two poles are genuinely the same beat.
It is not a licence to run short.

## Length

**41 to 61 words, median 45.** Under 41 the scene has no detail in it; over 61 a sentence is
doing work another sentence already did. If you are over, the cut is almost always in
sentence 2 — a second example, a qualifying clause, an adjective pair where one adjective
would do.

## The north star: relatability

Users should see themselves. Relatability comes from **concrete, recognizable behaviour**,
not abstract trait-naming. The "how did they know that's me" reaction is triggered by
everyday, low-stakes, faintly embarrassing situations — the unsent reply, the
over-rehearsed apology, the opinion that arrives three days late.

- **Show the bean doing the thing; don't label the disposition.** Behaviour is a mirror;
  adjectives are a diagnosis.
- **Use situations most people have actually lived** — social, emotional, mundane.
  Specificity reads as _truth_, not as "this is about someone else."
- **Sentences 3 and 4 are your best relatability levers.** People recognise their flaws
  faster than their virtues. Render them concretely and sympathetically — never clinically,
  and never as a reprimand.

## Voice

- **Third person** ("these Beans…", "they…") for the bean itself, always.
- **Second person is allowed for the other party in the scene** — _"how your grandmother met
  your grandfather"_, _"your new colleague"_, _"as you're falling asleep"_. 14 of the 79
  approved bodies do this, and it sharpens the mirror. What you may never do is address the
  reader _as_ the bean.
- Archetype-vignette, not horoscope-diagnosis.
- Contractions are normal — _doesn't_, _they've_, _isn't_. Use the uncontracted form when the
  sentence wants the extra beat (_"is advice they have never once taken"_), not by default.
- **Wry, never funny.** Dry and knowing, especially at sentences 3 and 4 — the affectionate
  raised eyebrow of someone who has seen this before and isn't going to make a thing of it.
  No jokes, no puns, no winking. The wit lives in _understatement_, in the gently
  undercutting turn at a sentence's end, in naming the small self-deception precisely and
  letting it sit. The reader should half-smile in recognition, not laugh. If a line is
  trying to be clever, cut the cleverness.
- **Em dashes are rare** — 4 bodies in 79. At this length a dash is usually a sentence that
  should have been split or trimmed.

## Banned moves

- **No "The challenge is [abstract noun]."** Dramatize the flaw.
- **No bean / flavour / form triad** — don't explain the entry as three semicolon-joined
  clauses, one per axis ("Adzuki Beans X; Bitter Beans Y; Boiled Beans Z"). It exposes the
  generative grammar. The combination is _felt_ in the portrait, never spelled out as inputs.
- **No abstract noun as the payload** ("their challenge is timeliness"). Convert every
  abstraction into a thing the reader has done.
- **No second paragraph**, and no fifth sentence.

## What actually carries the variety

Not the architecture — the architecture is fixed, and a reader who opens six entries in a
row is meant to feel the rhythm. Variety lives in:

- **the setting and the object** of sentence 2, which should not repeat anything nearby;
- **how sentence 3 opens** — `Sometimes` is the default, so earn a different entry where
  you can;
- **the tail of sentence 4**, which must be unlike every other tail in the corpus;
- **the register the trait implies** — a _meticulous_ bean's sentences can run precise and
  itemised; an _irrepressible_ one's can run fast and over the top of itself.

## Worked examples

Each is an approved body, annotated with the tier its sentences carry. Note that sentence 2
is never a definition, sentence 3 is always exactly one sentence, and sentence 4 always
fronts the behaviour before the incapacity.

**`sour-fried-cannellini`** — meticulous / obsessive / sloppy

> Agrodolce Cannellini Beans are the Bean Zodiac's most meticulous. `[opener]`
> Every wire is labelled, every version numbered, and when something breaks at midnight the
> fix takes four minutes. `[trait — the concrete instance, with the number doing the work]`
> The labelling can become the whole evening. `[excess — one sentence, no Sometimes]`
> Leaving a thing half-tidy and walking away is not restful for them. `[inverse — fronted,
> then the incapacity]`

**`bitter-dried-edamame`** — terse / brusque / verbose

> Desiccated Edamame are the Bean Zodiac's most terse. `[opener — note the plural: no
> "Beans"]`
> The reply is one line, the line answers it, the thread ends. `[trait — and the sentence
> itself is terse, which is the register point]`
> On a bad day it lands like a door closing. `[excess]`
> Two sentences of warmth first strikes them as time everyone would rather have back.
> `[inverse]`

**`bitter-smoked-kidney`** — haunting / harrowing / forgettable

> Charcoal Kidney Beans are the Bean Zodiac's most haunting. `[opener]`
> Something they said at a bus stop years ago still arrives, unasked, as you're falling
> asleep. `[trait — second person for the other party, which is what makes it land]`
> Sometimes what stays is the thing you would rather have put down. `[excess]`
> Being forgotten by anyone is not a risk they run. `[inverse]`

## Why the body must be rewritten when the triple changes

The opener names the trait, sentence 2 dramatises it, sentence 3 is the excess and sentence
4 is the inverse. So a body is not decoration on the frontmatter — it is the same five-tier
axis every `facet*`, `fortune*`, `answer*` and `rorschach*` runs, compressed into four
sentences. Change any word of the triple and the whole body is describing someone else.
