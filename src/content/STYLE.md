# Zodiac writing style

## Voice

Almanac and recipe-headnote, not horoscope. Think M.F.K. Fisher, Patience Gray, old farmers' almanacs — declarative, sensory, a little severe. Stay inside the kitchen-and-garden vocabulary: steep, render, blister, slake, fold, cure, simmer. Avoid modern register slipping in (`energy`, `vibe`, `manifest`, `journey`, `align`).

## Fortunes (the six lines: `seasonalFortune` + five dailies)

**Enact the trait, never name it.** A `judicious` zodiac never says "you are patient today" — it shows yuzu being held back from the pot. The reader should infer the trait from the gesture. It should be obvious that the fortunes are about the trait (or opposite of the trait if dailyLow or dailyLeast).

**dailyLow and dailyLeast are _positive expressions of the opposite trait_, not failure.** They are not scolding; the ingredient does not "fail." If the trait is `judicious`, low/least read as spontaneous-as-virtue (yuzu added early, soup unexpectedly bright). If the trait is `courageous`, low/least read as cautious-as-virtue. Same beanprint of ingredients as the high end — same dish, viewed from the opposite disposition.

**Vary cadence within the set of five.** Don't open every daily with "The X…". Mix declarative sentences, fragments, semicolon-balanced clauses, participial phrases collapsing into an image. Symmetric scaffolding reads as machine-generated even when it isn't.

**One image per fortune.** Two metaphors competing in 18 words muddies the line. Pick one and let it land.

**Clarity test (non-negotiable):** every line must paraphrase into a plain cooking instruction or a plain observation about the trait. "The broth knows itself" fails. "The broth is given time to deepen" passes. Poetry sits on top of a real, parseable thought; if the literal layer is missing, the line is murky, not poetic.

## Sharpening a line that is already correct

A fortune can pass the rules above and still read as flat. Reach for these in a second pass:

- **Specificity only this dish would have.** Generic kitchen verbs (skim, taste, stir) are scaffolding; anchor at least one line with a detail unique to these ingredients — the grey scum mung throws in the first ten minutes, the waxy squeak of bitter melon under salt, the chalk at a bean's centre when it hasn't soaked long enough. Observed beats composed.
- **Stakes inside the image.** Prefer gestures that imply consequence if skipped. "The cook who skips a step rebuilds it from the bottom" works because failure is visible. Aim for one or two lines per set where the cost of getting it wrong is legible in the picture.
- **Vary sentence length aggressively.** A four-word fragment next to a long semicolon-balanced clause creates rhythm. One very short line in a set of five lands hard; five medium-length lines blur together.
- **Trust the reader on low/least.** Cut adjectives that tell the reader how to feel about the result (`honestly itself`, `surprising and good`, `unexpectedly bright`). The image alone should carry the verdict — a rough soup eaten standing at the stove says everything without naming the mood.

## What to avoid

- Naming the trait outright in any fortune.
- Opening every daily with the same article + noun structure.
- dailyLow/Least depicting the ingredient failing or the reader being scolded.
- Abstract nouns standing in for images (`patience`, `courage`, `discernment`).
- Modern self-help vocabulary.
- Lines that sound poetic but cannot be paraphrased into a plain meaning.
