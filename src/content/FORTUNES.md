# Fortune-writing guide

This guide is for rewriting the five `fortune*` fields (`fortuneMost`, `fortuneHigh`, `fortuneMid`, `fortuneLow`, `fortuneLeast`) inside each zodiac frontmatter. Facets, body paragraphs, dish, quote, and seasonalFortune are **not** in scope — leave them alone.

## What a fortune is

The user reads one facet for their sign, says whether it resonates, and on resonance receives a fortune (does not necessarily match the facet). So a fortune is a tiny piece of advice or noticing for someone who has just been mirrored. It needs to land in one read.

## The rung system

Each sign has a trait (e.g. _judicious_, _competitive_, _hushed_). Fortunes sit on a five-rung scale of how strongly the user leans into that trait:

- **Most** — user is deeply in the trait. Fortune generally reinforces and aims it.
- **High** — user is strongly in it. Reinforce, but usually with a small turn or caveat.
- **Mid** — user is moderately in it. Often the most surprising rung — see below.
- **Low** — user is barely in it. Fortune counsels _against_ the trait, or affirms its absence.
- **Least** — user is the opposite. Fortune leans fully into that opposite.

The old fortunes followed this slider too mechanically — Most was "do it fully," Mid was always "trust the middle," Least was always a flat inversion. **Break that predictability.** Especially at Mid: let it sometimes name a specific, surprising move rather than a balanced platitude. The reader should not be able to guess the next rung from the previous one.

## Voice rules

1. **No bean, dish, or cooking imagery in fortunes.** That work lives in the facets and the body. Fortunes use plain everyday imagery — doors, kettles, weather, messages, light, walks, rooms, phones, scoreboards, evenings.
2. **Concrete over abstract.** Reach for things you can touch or hours of the day. Avoid abstract conceptual nouns like _judgment, commitment, openness, potency, charge_. Replace with verbs and objects: _send it, pick up the phone, before noon, the next room._
3. **Mix grammatical moods.** Across a sign's five fortunes, do not write five imperatives. Mix:
   - imperatives ("Send the three words.")
   - predictions ("Someone will ask twice today.")
   - conditionals ("If nothing arrives by evening...")
   - observations ("The room is louder than you are today.")
   - two-beat turns (image + consequence, or setup + small reversal)
     Aim for at least three different moods across the five rungs.
4. **Vary sentence shape.** Don't lean on em-dashes and semicolons every line. Some fortunes should be one short sentence; some two; some a sentence followed by a clipped fragment.
5. **Give each sign a tonal fingerprint.** Pick a register that suits the trait and hold it across all five rungs. Examples used in the pilot:
   - _potent_ → dry, confident, understated
   - _uncomplicated_ → plain, matter-of-fact, slightly warm
   - _ghostly_ → wistful, slightly conspiratorial
   - _competitive_ → sharp, wry, half-amused at the reader
   - _hushed_ → quiet, careful, with a held edge
     Other useful registers in the bank: tender, blunt, observational, gently teasing, deadpan, encouraging-without-being-soft.
6. **Earn a small surprise per fortune.** A turn, a noticing, an unexpected specific. The old fortunes resolved in the direction you expected; new ones should make the reader pause for a beat.
7. **Scene anchors are free flavour, but keep them perennial.** "Before noon," "by evening," "the second time," "in the next room," "the next message" — these make the fortune feel grounded in a moment. **Avoid "today," "tonight," "tomorrow," "this week," or any explicit framing of the present day.** The fortune is read on a particular day, but it should feel like it could apply whenever the reader meets the moment it describes. Perennial, not dated.

## Length

One sentence is fine. Two short sentences is fine. Don't exceed two. Watch for runaway clauses with semicolons — usually one of the halves is the real fortune and the other is filler.

## Common failure modes to avoid

- **Naked imperatives** ("Render your judgment.", "Disarm completely.") — abstract verb + abstract noun. Replace with a touchable action.
- **The "X is not Y; it is Z" turn** — overused in the original set.
- **Restating the trait name.** Don't write "Be competitive" for a competitive sign. The reader already knows. Show the move, not the label.
- **The Mid platitude** ("Find the balance," "Trust the middle," "It is enough"). If the Mid fortune could apply to any sign, rewrite it.
- **Tonal drift inside one sign.** If Most is wry, Least shouldn't be earnest. Pick a voice and hold it.
- **Generic urgency.** "Do it now" is weaker than "before the kettle cools" or "before the next message arrives."
- **Dated framing.** "Today," "tonight," "tomorrow," "this week." The fortune should feel perennial even though it's read on a specific day.
- **Relating to facet.** The fortune received might not necessarily be related to the matching facet. Do not relate the two concepts.
- **Cross-sign sameness.** Each sign's voice is supposed to be distinct, but it's easy to drift into a shared house style — the same openers ("Someone will ask...", "Send it before..."), the same anchors (doors, kettles, evenings, messages, drafts, "the room will adjust"), the same two-beat rhythm ("Do X. Y is Z."). If you can swap one sign's fortune into another sign's file without it feeling wrong, the voices are too close. After drafting a sign, scan the other signs you've already written and check for repeated openers, repeated objects, and repeated sentence shapes. The safe palette in rule 1 is a floor, not a ceiling — give each trait its own small object set (a jeweller's loupe for _exquisite_, a kettle and fists for _fierce_, receipts and forms for _uncompromising_, coffee and open windows for _acerbic_) and let those carry the tonal fingerprint.

## Pilot examples (use as calibration)

These five signs have already been rewritten and are the reference for the new voice.

### umami-dried-green — _potent_ (dry, confident, understated)

- **Most:** One message before noon. That's the day's whole quota — anything else dilutes it.
- **High:** You'll be tempted to follow it up. Don't. The first one is louder than you think.
- **Mid:** Aim once, then leave the room. The aftermath isn't a place you need to stand in.
- **Low:** The moment wants depth, not voltage. Sit longer with one thing instead of cutting it short.
- **Least:** If nothing arrives ready by evening, let the day end small. A quiet one in this run is doing its own kind of work.

### sweet-dried-edamame — _uncomplicated_ (plain, matter-of-fact, slightly warm)

- **Most:** Three words. Send them. You'll feel the urge to soften them — that's how you know they're right.
- **High:** Someone will ask a complicated question. The simple answer is the kind one.
- **Mid:** A clean reply closes something that has been quietly open for weeks.
- **Low:** The short version is reading as cold. Add the sentence you cut — that one was load-bearing.
- **Least:** Tell the whole knotted version. Whatever you usually trim off is the part they came for.

### sweet-smoked-pinto — _ghostly_ (wistful, slightly conspiratorial)

- **Most:** Walk into the middle of it, not the edge. The room will adjust around you faster than you expect.
- **High:** Someone is still quoting a thing you said weeks ago. Find out who, and answer them directly.
- **Mid:** Leave something with your name on it before you go — not a hint, an actual note.
- **Low:** Pick up the phone. The atmosphere does not need any more help; you do.
- **Least:** Stay till the end — coat off, last to leave. The disappearing trick has stopped reading as mystery.

### bitter-roasted-green — _competitive_ (sharp, wry, half-amused at the reader)

- **Most:** There is one scoreboard worth watching and you already know which one. Run.
- **High:** You'll catch yourself comparing twice before noon. The second time, ask what winning would actually look like.
- **Mid:** Close the gap by an inch, not the whole length. Leave something to work with later.
- **Low:** This one is a draw on purpose. Decline the rematch — the trophy is small and the dust is loud.
- **Least:** A race is happening in the next room. It isn't yours. Notice that you noticed, then go back to your own afternoon.

### spicy-smoked-butter — _hushed_ (quiet, careful, with a held edge)

- **Most:** Say less than you planned, but say it earlier. The calm is doing the work; it only needs a smaller signal to land.
- **High:** One sentence, sent before dark, lets the pressure out without anyone noticing it was there.
- **Mid:** Someone will ask if you're alright. Answer with something true, not something polite.
- **Low:** You have been turning the volume down on a thing that needs to be heard at full. Raise it.
- **Least:** The room is louder than you are, and that's correct. Match it, even if it runs hotter than your usual temperature.

## Process for an agent updating a sign

1. Read the full zodiac file. Note the trait, the five facet titles + lines, and the body paragraphs — these tell you what the sign is _about_ and what register fits.
2. Pick a tonal fingerprint for the voice. Write it down before drafting.
3. Draft all five fortunes together so the rungs read as a coherent scale with variety in mood and shape.
4. Self-check against the rules above. Specifically: any bean/dish imagery? Any rung that sounds like a template? More than two imperatives in a row? Any abstract conceptual verb (render, commit, disarm, offer)?
5. Edit only the five `fortune*` lines in frontmatter. Do not touch facets, body, dish, quote, or seasonalFortune.
