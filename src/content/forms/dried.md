Drying is reduction. The moisture that made the Bean soft and immediate is drawn out slowly until what remains is dense, light, inert — capable of sitting undisturbed on a shelf for years. Nothing is added. Only what is inessential is taken away.

Dried Beans carry a concentrated self that circumstance has not been able to dilute. Alone among the Forms, the Dried Bean undergoes nothing — not the pot, not the oil, not its own interior. It waits, intact, for as long as waiting takes.

The Dried Bean's shadow is brittleness. What cannot yield will eventually fracture. The Dried Season asks you to remember that softness, under the right conditions, is always possible.
